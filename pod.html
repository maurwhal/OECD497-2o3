Natsch Point-of-Departure Calculator

A Marvelous Maura Model • by Maura Lavelle
Reproduces the regression-based PoD models from Natsch, "Integrated Skin Sensitization Assessment Based on OECD Methods (III): Adding Human Data to the Potency Assessment", ALTEX, doi:10.14573/altex.2302081s3 (Supplementary Material ESM3).
← Hub 2o3 Hazard tool
This is not the SARA-ICE tool. SARA-ICE (NICEATM/Unilever) uses a proprietary Bayesian model calibrated on 400+ ICE chemicals whose coefficients are not published, so it can't be reproduced here. This calculator instead reproduces the fully-published Natsch et al. linear regression models exactly as given in ESM3. Use the two "Load worked example" buttons below and check the Self-check panel to confirm this page reproduces the source spreadsheet's own examples before trusting it on a real material — this exists specifically so you don't have to take my arithmetic on faith.

Always check the Sources panel before trusting the filled values.

All three fields (EC1.5, EC3.0, IC50) are needed for any KeratinoSens-based model below — a partial set is treated as not sufficient data rather than guessed. No induction/cytotoxicity reached at top concentration → enter 4000 mM (the ESM3 default), not blank.
kDPRA (TG 442C)
Chemicals tested but non-reactive: enter -3.5, per ESM3.
h-CLAT (TG 442E)
All three fields (CD86, CD54, CV75) plus MW are needed for any h-CLAT-based model below. No induction/cytotoxicity reached → enter 5000 (the ESM3 default), not blank. MIT is auto-computed as min(CD86,CD54).

Results — models trained on LLNA data (EC3)

pED = model's linear predictor; EC3% = MW / 10^pED; DSA = EC3% × 250 (dose per skin area, µg/cm²).

Results — models trained on extended Potency Values (PV)

Per ESM3's own guidance: a conservative assessment may take the lower PoD of the LLNA- and PV-trained models, unless there's a mechanistic reason to prefer one (e.g. a strongly cytotoxic chemical with weak alerts/reactivity — prefer the PV-trained model there to avoid a cytotoxicity-driven bias).
